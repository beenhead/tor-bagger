⛰️ Getting a GPX file out of Strava

A GPX file is just the list of GPS points your watch or phone recorded on a walk. Upload one to Tor Bagger and it works out which Tors you passed.

← Back to the map

One walk at a time

You need a computer, or a phone browser set to "desktop site". The Strava mobile app has no export button — this only exists on the website.

  1. Go to strava.com and log in.
  2. Open the activity you want. You can only export your own activities, not a friend's.
  3. Find the ··· menu (labelled something like "More options") on the activity page — usually top-right, near the edit and share buttons.
  4. Choose Export GPX. The file downloads straight away.
  5. Back on the Tor Bagger map, hit Choose file under GPS Upload, pick the .gpx you just downloaded, then Analyze Hike.
Shortcut: add /export_gpx to the end of an activity's web address and the download starts immediately — no menus. For example:
https://www.strava.com/activities/1234567890/export_gpx

If there's no "Export GPX" option

Activities uploaded from a Garmin, Wahoo or similar sometimes offer Export Original instead. That gives you the file exactly as the device recorded it, which is often a .fit or .tcx rather than a .gpx.

Tor Bagger only reads .gpx, so you'll need to convert it. Any of the free GPS file converters will do it in a browser — search for "fit to gpx converter". Alternatively, if the activity also synced to Garmin Connect, export it from there as GPX and skip the conversion.

Your whole Strava history at once

Worth doing if you've been walking Dartmoor for years and want it all bagged in one go. Don't unzip it — Tor Bagger reads the archive as it comes.

  1. On strava.com, go to Settings → My Account.
  2. Click Download or Delete Your Account, then Get Started.
  3. Under the "Download Request" step, click Request Your Archive. Ignore the delete options further down the page.
  4. Strava emails you a download link. It can take anywhere from a few minutes to a day or two.
  5. On the Tor Bagger map, under Strava Bulk Import, choose the .zip and hit Import Archive. Leave the tab open while it works through your walks.
Only your walks are read. Tor Bagger looks at the archive's activity list and picks out the walks, hikes and runs — rides and everything else are ignored, on the basis that a Tor counts when you get there on foot. Your photos and videos are never opened and never leave your computer: the zip is unpacked in your browser, and only the walking tracks are sent on.
These archives are big — often several hundred MB, mostly photos. Picking it straight from your Downloads folder is fine; there's no need to move or extract it first.

Not a Strava user?

Any app that records a walk can usually export GPX:

  • Garmin Connect — open the activity, then the gear or ··· menu → Export to GPX.
  • OS Maps — open the route or recorded activity and look for the download / export option.
  • Komoot — on a completed tour, ··· → Export GPX.

What Tor Bagger does with it

  • Any Tor you came within 150 m of gets bagged automatically.
  • Come within 500 m but no closer and it's logged as a near miss — so you know what to go back for.
  • Each bag is dated from the timestamp on the GPS point itself, so old walks land on the right date rather than today.
  • Tors you've already bagged are skipped, so it's safe to upload the same file twice.
Privacy zones: if you have one set up in Strava around your home, the exported file has the start and end of the walk trimmed off. A Tor inside that trimmed section won't be bagged — you'd need to log it from the map instead.
Strava moves its buttons around from time to time, so the exact wording may differ from the above — the export option lives on the activity page either way.
